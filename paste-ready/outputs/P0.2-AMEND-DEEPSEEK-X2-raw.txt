**Findings ranked**

**BLOCKER 1 — UP-41 understates the Fyers licence conflict and misreads its reach.**
The row says Fyers terms sections 11–12 "bear on" Model B and that section 11's first limb "reaches further than the list." The actual Fyers API terms section 2 permit personal-use private interfaces or platforms offered to other *Fyers* clients after approvals; section 3 permits access only by documented means; section 8 allows onward platforms only to other Fyers clients after exchange/statutory approvals . The extract's own legal note concedes that sections 2, 8 and 9 are "no route for Model B subscribers at other brokers." That is not a nuance; it is a structural bar. The minimal fix: UP-41 must state plainly that Fyers API terms do not authorise a multi-broker signals product for non-Fyers subscribers at all, not merely that consent may be needed. OQ-68 should be scoped to "can Fyers be used at all under Model B," not "what do sections 11–12 mean."

**BLOCKER 2 — UP-40's conclusion that "free multi-year intraday exists still holds" is false on the cited sources.**
UP-40 says the #68 figure "may have been right when read" and that the conclusion survives through Upstox from January 2022. Fyers' own plans page states Standard intraday is **1 year (from 2025)**, rolling annually; Prime intraday is **5+ years** . Fyers' product page repeats "1 years (from 2025)" for Standard . The extract's own UP-40 cites F-77/F-78 for exactly this. The failure is that the row then treats the Owner's "free intraday from 2017" as a premise failure that can be cured by switching vendor. It cannot: the Owner's decision recorded Fyers as the intraday source **and recorded it as free**. Upstox is a different vendor with a different archive start (January 2022). That is a new decision, not a correction. The minimal fix: UP-40 must say the Owner's 2026-10-17 decision is **factually wrong on the free Fyers intraday archive** and requires the Owner to either (a) accept Upstox from 2022 as a substituted primary, or (b) pay for Prime, or (c) reopen #68. It must not say the conclusion "still holds."

**HIGH 1 — BQ-50's DEFAULT-61 assumes Fyers "has no scheduled job" without addressing the documented 2FA/refresh conflict correctly.**
The row cites F-103 (Fyers notice of 2026-03-10) for "continuous refresh-token sessions are no longer supported" and says that notice "addresses automated trading strategies." That is accurate as far as it goes: the Fyers SEBI framework notice says "Users will need to complete 2FA once every trading day. Continuous refresh-token sessions will no longer be supported" . But the extract then treats the non-trading-data-app question as open (OQ-74) while DEFAULT-61 already assumes no scheduled job. The failure: if the notice's 2FA requirement is read as applying to **API access generally** (the notice says "using the FYERS API or third-party platforms"), then even a non-trading data pull requires daily human 2FA. The extract's own UP-45 says "two brokers, two daily steps does not arise while Fyers has no scheduled job"—but if Fyers *does* have a human-started daily pull, it is still a daily human step. The minimal fix: state explicitly that under DEFAULT-61, Fyers data freshness is bounded by human login availability, and that a missed 06:30 token expiry means no Fyers data for that day. Do not present this as "no daily step arises."

**HIGH 2 — The research gate rests on a forum reply (F-100) as if it were a licence, and the extract knows it.**
The research gate permits a read-only client on a PROVISIONAL Upstox row "on the strength of F-100 (storage and derived analytics for informational use)." UP-41 itself says "a forum reply is not a licence." The gate then adds that "the research gate is an Owner-level reading of a forum reply." That is circular: the gate is authorised by the very thing the spec says is not authoritative. The minimal fix: either close the research gate until OQ-68 produces a written answer, or restate it as an **Owner-accepted risk** with no claim that F-100 licenses anything. The current wording lets a reader believe F-100 has legal weight it does not.

**HIGH 3 — The typed model `VendorSeriesSnapshot` contradicts DEFAULT-62 and UP-41 on Fyers deletion obligations.**
The model permits a Fyers snapshot with `adjustment_basis=VENDOR_ADJUSTED` and stores `content_sha256` for an immutable record. DEFAULT-62 says a snapshot is never overwritten. But the extract's own Fyers legal note cites section 15: "on termination use stops and stored content is deleted, which conflicts with keeping snapshots ([DEFAULT-62])." The typed model does not encode any deletion obligation or termination state. If OQ-68 ever permits Fyers, the model must carry a `retention_basis` or `termination_delete_required` flag. As written, the model silently assumes perpetual retention that the Fyers terms forbid. Minimal fix: add a `retention_obligation` enum (NONE, DELETE_ON_TERMINATION) and require Fyers snapshots to carry DELETE_ON_TERMINATION.

**MEDIUM 1 — PE-19's "more than 3 per-minute breaches block the user for the day" is not supported by the cited Fyers notice.**
The Fyers notice of 2026-10-14 sets Data API limits at 5/sec, 50/min, 5,000/day for Standard and 10/sec, 500/min, 5,00,000/day for Prime . It says "If an applicable limit is reached, further requests may be restricted until that limit resets" . It does **not** state a "3 breaches then day block" rule. The extract's PE-19 asserts this as fact. The minimal fix: mark the "more than 3 per-minute breaches" rule as UNVERIFIED or remove it until a source is found; the PE-19 behaviour should be "stop and wait one full window" only.

**MEDIUM 2 — BQ-48's Fyers Prime cost (INR 5,888.20 with GST) conflicts with the Fyers support page.**
The Fyers support page states Prime is **₹7,500 billed annually** . The extract says INR 5,888.20 a year with GST. The discrepancy is large enough to matter for the Owner's "no subscriptions" constraint (UP-46). A third-party article cites ₹4,990 annually , but Fyers' own support page is the authoritative vendor source. The minimal fix: correct the cost to ₹7,500/year (plus GST if applicable; the support page does not state GST treatment) or cite the exact Fyers page that supports ₹5,888.20. If no such page exists, the figure is wrong.

**MEDIUM 3 — The matrix scores Fyers Standard intraday at 5 for "history depth" while BQ-48 says a year cannot carry an event study.**
The intraday_bars / Fyers Standard row has a column value of 5 (presumably history depth) and a score of 41. BQ-48 says under (c) "a year or so cannot carry an event study." If the matrix column is history depth, a score of 5 is wrong; if it is something else, the column header is missing from the extract and the score is unauditable. The minimal fix: either correct the Fyers Standard intraday history-depth score to 1 (matching the extract's own assessment) or publish the column headers so the score can be checked. A reader cannot tell which column is which, and the score contradicts the prose.

**LOW 1 — The typed model `AccountActivity` excludes Upstox from dormancy providers only by omission.**
`DORMANCY_PROVIDERS = frozenset({ProviderId.UPSTOX, ProviderId.FYERS})` and the validator rejects any other provider. That is correct for the stated purpose. But PE-20 says Upstox dormancy may show as "token generation answers 'No segments for these users are active'" and that a machine assigns ACCOUNT_RESTRICTED "only on the Upstox message or code above." The model has no field for the error code. The dormancy guard is purely time-based (`last_activity_on`). The minimal fix: either state that `AccountActivity` is only the *time-based* alert and PE-20's code-based assignment is separate, or add a `restriction_code` field. As written, a reader may think `AccountActivity` covers PE-20; it does not.

**What the extract gets wrong about the Owner's decision of 2026-10-17**

The extract treats the decision as having two components: Upstox for daily history, Fyers for intraday, "both recorded as free." That much is accurate as a description of the recorded decision. What it gets wrong is the **status** of that decision.

First, it says UP-40's conclusion "still holds, through Upstox from January 2022." The Owner's decision did **not** select Upstox for intraday. It selected Fyers for intraday. Substituting Upstox is a **new decision**, not a survival of the old one. The extract's phrasing ("OQ-14 stays closed by a different vendor") makes a substitution sound like continuity.

Second, it says the #68 figure "may have been right when read." Fyers' Standard intraday window is "1 year (from 2025)" on the current plans page . If the Owner read "from 3 July 2017" on the same page, the page is internally inconsistent (the extract says this). But the extract does not say which reading the Owner adopted. The record should state: the Owner's decision of 2026-10-17 is **not reproducible from the current Fyers pages** without selecting Prime, which is paid and by waitlist (F-83).

Third, the extract correctly flags that UP-41's "personal system" framing conflicts with Model B. But it does not say what the Owner's decision **did** say about Model B. The decision was made **against** Model B (2026-10-14), per UP-41's own text: "Owner decision of 2026-10-17 against Model B (2026-10-14), BO-1, MB-AC-14." The extract then treats the decision as compatible with Model B by routing everything through two gates. That is backwards: the decision was against Model B. The gates may be a mitigation, but they do not make the decision compatible with Model B; they make it **provisional for the Owner's own research only**.

**Verdict: FIX-THEN-SHIP.**

The typed models are mostly sound: `VendorSeriesSnapshot` correctly forbids RAW for India and requires VENDOR_ADJUSTED for Fyers; `dormancy_alerts` correctly treats missing/bad input as `(0,)`; `AccountActivity` validates thresholds. The failure is not in the code; it is in the **prose rows overclaiming** what the Owner's decision establishes and what the gates authorise. Fix BLOCKER 1 (UP-41 plain statement), BLOCKER 2 (UP-40 no "still holds"), HIGH 2 (research gate is Owner risk, not F-100 licence), and HIGH 3 (retention flag) before ship. The matrix column headers must be published before the scores can be audited.
